Our approach

Built for understanding, not memorising

Whether the topic is a loop in Python or a machine in automata theory, fuzzy mental models get clearer when you can watch the idea unfold.

Step at your own pace

Advance one step at a time, step back, or press play. The active step is always highlighted so you never lose your place.

See state change

Variables, arrays, stacks — and later automata, gates, and other models — are drawn as labeled boxes that update as each step runs.

Plain-language explanations

Every step comes with a short explanation of what happened and why — including the traps students commonly hit.

For students

Build intuition before the exam — or the code

  • Replay any step as many times as you need
  • See how variables, structures, and models change
  • Keyboard shortcuts for fast stepping
For teachers

Project it, share a link, done

  • No accounts, installs or plugins — just a URL
  • Each simulation is a single page that works offline
  • Designed to be readable on a classroom projector
Open source

Help us build the next simulation

Every simulation is a plain HTML page with no build step. Pick a programming or theory concept, add a folder under simulations/, and open a pull request.
